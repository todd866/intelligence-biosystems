# 2026 author edition

This folder contains a full rewrite of Ian Todd’s “Intelligence as high-dimensional coherence: The observable dimensionality bound and computational tractability”, published in *BioSystems* 260 (2026), 105704, [doi:10.1016/j.biosystems.2026.105704](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biosystems.2026.105704).

The published article remains the version of record and the one to cite. This edition is a separate account of how the author would present the argument today. The published text and its accompanying materials are unchanged.

Intelligence is the maintenance and adaptive use of high-dimensional coherent dynamics that outrun tracking through limited observation channels. The organism embodies the process that an observer must reconstruct. Continuous substrates carry these dynamics with sparse irreversible registrations, avoiding the repeated overwrites involved in keeping a discrete record of the evolving state.

The argument now leads with this thesis and develops it through biology: a hand adjusting its grip, chemotactic memory, neural population dynamics and bioelectric coordination. Metastability preserves history; selective coherence keeps useful distinctions available; constraint geometry turns those relations into action. Sparse readout and reusable codes explain how compact outputs recruit extensive internal organisation.

The structure is sharper and the mathematics works alongside the biology. The channel-relative tracking bound has a direct numerical example: 300 independently renewed four-state modes require 6,000 bits/s, while a 100-bit/s channel supports five. Participation ratio measures distributed variance rather than feature count. The energy account distinguishes acquisition, retention, reset and ongoing dissipation. Relaxation exploits the encoded objective, and compatible learning stabilises reusable codes. Each result carries its conditions where it is developed.

The shorter exposition builds toward experiments that change the relations sustaining flexible action and measure the consequences. It retains every reference in this edition’s bibliography and introduces no new experimental results.
